# Competitive analysis — resource & staffing planning tools

Harbor & Pine's actual problem (three spreadsheets, Monday morning, "who's open, who's overloaded, where does the next project go") already has a market of dedicated software trying to solve it. Worth knowing what exists before designing as if nothing does. Six products below, chosen because they span the range Harbor & Pine would realistically evaluate: from a 25-person practice up through a 100-person firm deciding whether to outgrow spreadsheets.

| Product | Best fit | Staffing-relevant strength | Where it falls short of this problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Float](https://www.float.com)** | Agencies/studios, visual-first teams | Drag-and-drop schedule, utilization reporting, Smart Assign (ML-based role-fit suggestion, shipped March 2026), strong budget/billable-hours reporting | Scheduling-first tool — staffing a specific open role against ranked candidates isn't really its workflow; it's built around dragging a known person onto a known slot, not comparing several unknown candidates for one slot |
| **[Runn](https://www.runn.io)** | Consultancies forecasting demand | Tentative-vs-confirmed projects as a first-class concept (directly validates the Confirmed/Tentative split this prototype uses), bird's-eye calendar surfacing double-bookings, scenario modeling | Scenario modeling is powerful but adds a planning layer most 25-person practices don't need every Monday — overkill for "who do I put on this role right now" |
| **[Resource Guru](https://resourceguru.com)** | Small-to-mid teams, simple booking | Clash management flags double-bookings in real time, waiting list for tentative work, leave management baked in | Reporting is thinner than Float/Runn; little in the way of skills-based candidate comparison — booking is still manual lookup, not ranked matching |
| **[Forecast](https://www.forecast.app)** | Teams wanting AI auto-scheduling | Auto-schedules tasks against availability using AI | Independent reviews score it lower than Float on resource definition and capacity (8.1/8.6 vs 7.3/7.0 per G2) — the AI auto-assign is a black box in exactly the place this prototype deliberately avoids one |
| **[Kantata](https://www.kantata.com)** (formerly Mavenlink) | 100–5,000 consultant PSA shops | Algorithmic staffing-plan optimization across dozens of parameters, deep demand forecasting, AI-assisted planning | Built for operations/delivery leaders at firms far larger than Harbor & Pine's 100-person scale; the depth is real but the tool (and its price) assumes a PMO, not one Resource Manager making a Monday call |
| **[Productive.io](https://productive.io)** | Agencies needing resourcing + financials in one | Tentative bookings that don't touch capacity until confirmed, a dedicated "resource request" flow (shipped March 2026) for Delivery-Manager-style asks, bookings tied directly to budget/margin | Resourcing lives behind a general-purpose project/finance tool — getting to "who's open this week" takes more clicks than a purpose-built Monday view would need |

## What's already validated by the market

- **Tentative-vs-confirmed as a first-class state** (Runn, Productive) is not a novel idea — it's table stakes in every serious competitor. That's reassuring, not a reason to cut it: it confirms the problem statement's recommendation (keep Tentative load visible, never merged into committed totals) matches where the category has already converged, not a speculative design bet.
- **Clash/overallocation flagging in real time** (Resource Guru, Runn) is also standard. The prototype's red-at-100%-not-101% threshold and non-blocking overallocation warning are consistent with that pattern, not inventing one.

## Where this prototype's bets differ from the category

- **No blended match score, anywhere in the category I found.** Float's Smart Assign and Forecast's auto-scheduler both move toward *automated* candidate selection — a system that decides who fits and surfaces a suggestion. This prototype deliberately goes the other way: three separate visible facts (skills, availability, level), sorted but never collapsed into one number or one auto-suggestion. For a firm this size, where the Resource Manager has to be able to explain a staffing call to a client or a Delivery Manager by name, a black-box "best match" is a liability, not a convenience. That's the clearest point of disagreement with where the market leaders (Float, Forecast) are headed.
- **Plan-vs-actual divergence as a visible, per-candidate flag.** None of the six surfaced this as a dedicated UI pattern in the research above — the category mostly treats logged time as a separate timesheet/reporting concern (Runn, Productive), not something shown inline at the moment of a staffing decision. Surfacing it on the candidate row itself, at exactly the moment someone's about to be assigned based on a plan number that might be stale, is the one idea here I didn't find a direct precedent for.
- **Scale fit.** Kantata is the one competitor built for something like Harbor & Pine's *parent* organization (100 people), but its staffing tools assume an operations layer this practice doesn't have. Float/Runn/Resource Guru are sized right for the 25-person practice but don't model the specific "Monday, one Resource Manager, one decision" moment as tightly as this prototype's scope does — they're built as always-on schedulers, not a focused staffing-flow a time-boxed Monday review.
